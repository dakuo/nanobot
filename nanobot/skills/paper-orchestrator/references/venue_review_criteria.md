# Venue Review Criteria

Review dimensions, scoring scales, and process specifics for each supported venue.

---

## CHI (ACM Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems)

**Scoring scale:** 1.0 – 5.0 (half-point increments)
- 5.0 = Best paper quality, accept without hesitation
- 4.0 = Strong accept, clear contribution
- 3.0 = Borderline, interesting but notable weaknesses
- 2.0 = Weak, significant issues
- 1.0 = Reject, fundamental flaws

**Review dimensions:**
1. **Novelty and Originality**: Does this paper present new ideas, methods, or findings?
2. **Significance and Impact**: Will this influence future research or practice?
3. **Rigor and Validity**: Is the methodology sound? Are claims supported by evidence?
4. **Presentation Quality**: Is the paper well-written, well-organized, and clear?
5. **Relevance to CHI**: Does this fit the CHI community's interests?
6. **Reviewer Expertise**: Self-assessed (Knowledgeable / Passing Knowledge / No Knowledge)
7. **Reviewer Confidence**: Self-assessed (High / Medium / Low)

**What CHI values most:** Novelty in interaction techniques, strong empirical evidence, real-world impact, interdisciplinary approaches bridging computing and human behavior.

**Common acceptance reasons:** Novel contribution with solid evaluation, timely topic with rigorous methods, system contribution with meaningful user insights.

**Common rejection reasons:** Incremental contribution, weak evaluation, overclaiming from insufficient evidence, poor writing quality, mismatch with CHI scope.

**Subcommittee structure:** ~20 subcommittees covering areas like Accessibility, AI/ML in HCI, Design, Games, Health, Learning, Privacy/Security, Social Computing, Visualization, etc. Papers are assigned to subcommittees based on topic keywords. Each subcommittee has its own ACs and review norms.

**R&R process:** CHI moved to a revise-and-resubmit model in 2024. First round reviews in ~6 weeks. R&R decisions give authors ~4-5 weeks to revise. One round of R&R (no second revision cycle). Authors submit response letter + revised paper.

**Page/word limits:** No strict page limit since 2024 (previously 10 pages + references). However, reviewers expect concise papers, and 8-12 pages of content is typical. Excessive length is penalized informally.

## CSCW (ACM Conference on Computer-Supported Cooperative Work)

**Scoring scale:** 1.0 – 5.0 (same as CHI)
- Also uses Accept / Revise & Resubmit / Reject decisions

**Review dimensions:**
1. **Contribution**: Does the paper make a clear, significant contribution?
2. **Novelty**: Is the work sufficiently novel relative to prior CSCW literature?
3. **Method**: Is the research methodology appropriate and well-executed?
4. **Presentation**: Is the paper clearly written and well-structured?
5. **Relevance**: Does the work address social or collaborative computing?

**What CSCW values most:** Deep understanding of social dynamics in technology use, longitudinal studies, mixed-methods approaches, real-world deployments, attention to equity and justice.

**Common acceptance reasons:** Rich qualitative insights about collaborative practices, novel social computing systems with field deployments, strong mixed-methods designs.

**Common rejection reasons:** Lab study that ignores social context, system without deployment, superficial treatment of social dimensions.

**Subcommittee structure:** Smaller than CHI (~8-10 areas). Focused on social computing, collaboration, online communities, future of work, health collaboration, crisis informatics.

**R&R process:** CSCW has a journal-like review process (published in PACM HCI). Multiple R&R rounds are possible (typically 1-2). First review ~8 weeks. R&R gives ~6-8 weeks per round. Authors can improve substantially across rounds.

**Page/word limits:** No strict page limit (journal format). Typical papers are 20-30 pages in PACM HCI format. Reviewers accept longer papers if the length is justified.

## UIST (ACM Symposium on User Interface Software and Technology)

**Scoring scale:** Strong Reject / Reject / Borderline / Accept / Strong Accept (also 1-5 numeric)

**Review dimensions:**
1. **Technical Novelty**: Is the interaction technique, system, or algorithm novel?
2. **Technical Quality**: Is the implementation sound and well-evaluated?
3. **Significance**: Does this advance the state of the art in UI software/technology?
4. **Presentation**: Is the paper and accompanying video/demo compelling?
5. **Reproducibility**: Could another researcher replicate this work?

**What UIST values most:** Technical innovation in interaction, novel input/output techniques, creative systems that push boundaries, strong demos and videos, elegant engineering.

**Common acceptance reasons:** Novel interaction technique with convincing demo, significant technical contribution with thorough evaluation, creative system that opens new design space.

**Common rejection reasons:** Incremental improvement to known technique, no technical depth (design study without technical contribution), poor demo/video quality.

**Subcommittee structure:** No formal subcommittees. Single PC with broad expertise in input, output, fabrication, accessibility, AR/VR, toolkits, and programming.

**R&R process:** UIST does not have R&R. Accept/reject decisions are final. Shepherding may be offered for conditionally accepted papers (minor revisions supervised by AC).

**Page/word limits:** 10 pages + references (ACM format). Supplementary materials including video are strongly encouraged.

## UbiComp / IMWUT (ACM on Interactive, Mobile, Wearable and Ubiquitous Technologies)

**Scoring scale:** Reject / Major Revision / Minor Revision / Accept

**Review dimensions:**
1. **Novelty**: Does this present new sensing, interaction, or system concepts?
2. **Technical Soundness**: Are the algorithms, systems, or studies well-executed?
3. **Evaluation**: Is the evaluation appropriate (technical benchmarks + user studies)?
4. **Real-world Applicability**: Does this work in realistic conditions, not just controlled settings?
5. **Presentation**: Is the writing clear and well-organized?

**What UbiComp values most:** Novel sensing and context-aware systems, robust technical evaluations with real-world data, wearable/mobile/IoT applications, longitudinal deployments, attention to ecological validity.

**Common acceptance reasons:** Novel sensing modality with rigorous evaluation, system deployed in the wild with meaningful data, strong technical contribution with user validation.

**Common rejection reasons:** Lab-only evaluation for a system claiming real-world applicability, insufficient technical evaluation (no baselines, small dataset), incremental sensing improvement.

**Subcommittee structure:** No subcommittees. Rolling submission with quarterly deadlines (Feb, May, Aug, Nov). Each paper assigned to 3-4 reviewers by AEs.

**R&R process:** IMWUT has a journal model with rolling deadlines. Major Revision gives authors ~2 months. Minor Revision gives ~1 month. Papers can go through multiple revision cycles. Rejected papers can be resubmitted as new submissions to a future deadline.

**Page/word limits:** No strict page limit (journal format, published in PACM IMWUT). Typical papers are 20-28 pages. Excessive length without justification is noted by reviewers.

## DIS (ACM Designing Interactive Systems)

**Scoring scale:** 1.0 – 5.0 (same scale as CHI)

**Review dimensions:**
1. **Design Contribution**: Does this advance design knowledge, methods, or practice?
2. **Novelty**: Is the design approach, artifact, or framework new?
3. **Rigor**: Is the design process and/or evaluation well-executed?
4. **Relevance**: Does this address the design of interactive systems?
5. **Presentation**: Is the paper well-written with effective use of visuals?

**What DIS values most:** Design research contributions (not just empirical or engineering), research through design, design frameworks and patterns, critical and speculative design, participatory and co-design approaches, pictorial submissions.

**Common acceptance reasons:** Novel design framework grounded in practice, research through design with reflective analysis, design methods validated through real projects.

**Common rejection reasons:** System paper without design reflection, user study without design implications, purely technical contribution better suited for UIST/CHI.

**Subcommittee structure:** ~6-8 thematic areas including Design Methods, Design Theory, Participatory Design, Critical Design, Design for Health/Sustainability/Social Justice.

**R&R process:** DIS adopted R&R in recent years. Similar timeline to CHI: first reviews in ~6 weeks, R&R gives ~4 weeks. One revision round. Shepherding for conditionally accepted papers.

**Page/word limits:** 10 pages + references (ACM format). Pictorials have separate formatting (visual-heavy, reduced text). DIS also accepts shorter contributions (4-6 pages).
